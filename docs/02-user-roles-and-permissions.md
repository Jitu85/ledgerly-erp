# User Roles and Permissions

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the standard roles, permission model, data scope, approval controls, and segregation-of-duties expectations for the ERP platform.

Roles are permission bundles, not hard-coded exceptions. A company may create custom roles from granular permissions, subject to protected platform and ownership rules.

## 2. Permission Model

An authorization decision must consider all of the following:

1. **Tenant:** The active company must match the company that owns the resource.
2. **Module permission:** The role must allow the action, such as `sales.invoice.post`.
3. **Data scope:** The user must be allowed to access the branch, warehouse, department, employee group, or assigned records.
4. **Workflow state:** The requested action must be valid for the document's current state.
5. **Approval authority:** Amount, discount, margin, or other limits must be satisfied.
6. **Sensitive data rule:** Payroll, personal information, cost, margin, bank, and security data may need extra permission.
7. **Segregation rule:** A user may be prevented from approving or paying a transaction they created.

The server must enforce every rule. Hiding a button in the user interface is not an authorization control.

## 3. Permission Actions

Modules should use a consistent action vocabulary:

- `view`: See a list or record.
- `create`: Start a draft.
- `edit`: Change a draft or other editable state.
- `submit`: Send a record for approval.
- `approve` / `reject`: Decide a workflow step.
- `post`: Create final stock or accounting effects.
- `reverse`: Create an authorized reversal.
- `cancel`: Cancel a non-posted document.
- `delete`: Remove an allowed draft; posted records cannot be deleted.
- `export`: Download data.
- `import`: Upload structured data.
- `manage`: Configure master data or policy.
- `assign`: Allocate stock, work, roles, or scope.
- `close` / `reopen`: Control periods or operational cycles.

## 4. Standard Roles

### 4.1 Super Admin

**Scope:** Platform-wide, outside ordinary tenant roles.

**Responsibilities:**

- Manage registered companies, plans, subscriptions, and account status.
- Manage platform administrators and support roles.
- Review platform access logs, security events, job health, and incidents.
- Handle account recovery and controlled support access.
- Configure platform-wide security and retention settings.

**Restrictions:**

- Must not use unrestricted routine access to company business data.
- Support access must require a reason, be logged, and be time-limited where possible.
- Platform billing and security duties should be separated for larger operations.
- Super Admin actions must never appear as if performed by a company user.

### 4.2 Company Owner

**Scope:** One company, normally all branches and warehouses.

**Responsibilities:**

- Control company settings, administrators, subscription, and ownership.
- Approve high-risk or high-value actions.
- Review financial, operational, payroll summary, and audit reports.
- Delegate authority and approve sensitive configuration.

**Restrictions:**

- Ownership transfer, company deletion, and security reset require enhanced verification.
- Detailed payroll access may be disabled if the owner delegates confidential payroll duties.

### 4.3 Company Admin

**Scope:** One company; configurable branch and warehouse scope.

**Responsibilities:**

- Manage company users, custom roles, scope assignments, warehouses, product master data, payroll settings, and approval workflows.
- Configure document sequences, taxes, units, and operational settings.
- Monitor imports, integrations, and background jobs for the company.

**Restrictions:**

- Cannot become Super Admin.
- Cannot transfer company ownership unless expressly authorized.
- Should not automatically receive payroll details, bank credentials, or journal posting rights.

### 4.4 Accountant

**Scope:** One company and permitted branches or cost centres.

**Responsibilities:**

- Manage chart of accounts, journals, bills, invoices, receipts, payments, reconciliation, tax, periods, and financial reports.
- Review accounting entries generated by operations.
- Post authorized adjustments and reversals.

**Restrictions:**

- Cannot change physical stock without inventory permission and approval.
- Cannot alter posted operational or accounting records.
- Bank payment preparation and bank payment approval should be separable.

### 4.5 Purchase Manager

**Scope:** Assigned company, warehouses, branches, product categories, and approval limits.

**Responsibilities:**

- Manage suppliers, requisitions, quotations, purchase orders, expected receipts, and supplier returns.
- Review purchase prices and supplier performance.
- Approve purchases within assigned authority.

**Restrictions:**

- Cannot approve their own transaction where maker-checker policy applies.
- Cannot release payment unless separately granted.
- Cannot post stock receipt unless warehouse receiving permission is granted.

### 4.6 Sales Manager

**Scope:** Assigned company, branches, warehouses, sales teams, and approval limits.

**Responsibilities:**

- Manage customers, quotations, orders, pricing, allocations, invoices, returns, and sales reports.
- Approve discounts, credit exceptions, and returns within assigned authority.

**Restrictions:**

- Cannot approve their own exception where maker-checker policy applies.
- Cannot receive or refund money unless separately granted.
- Cost and margin visibility can be restricted.

### 4.7 Warehouse Staff

**Scope:** One or more assigned warehouses and locations.

**Responsibilities:**

- Receive, inspect, put away, pick, pack, dispatch, transfer, count, and record batch or serial information.
- Scan barcodes and print permitted labels.
- Report damage, expiry, and count differences.

**Restrictions:**

- Cannot change product conversions, prices, or accounting rules.
- Adjustments above a configured threshold require approval.
- Cannot see unrelated warehouses or confidential financial information.

### 4.8 HR/Payroll Officer

**Scope:** Assigned company, legal entity, branch, department, or employee group.

**Responsibilities:**

- Manage employee records, shifts, attendance, leave, payroll settings, payroll runs, payslips, and payroll reports.
- Prepare payroll and authorized payment files.

**Restrictions:**

- Salary and personal data are confidential.
- Payroll preparation, approval, and bank release should be separable.
- Cannot post the payroll journal unless accounting permission is granted.

### 4.9 Employee

**Scope:** Own records and explicitly assigned team actions.

**Responsibilities:**

- View and update allowed profile fields.
- Clock attendance or submit timesheets.
- Request leave, submit expenses, and view payslips.
- Managers may approve direct reports' requests if separately permitted.

**Restrictions:**

- Cannot view another employee's salary or private records.
- Cannot approve their own leave, attendance correction, or expense claim.

### 4.10 Read-only Auditor

**Scope:** Assigned company, date range, modules, branches, and warehouses.

**Responsibilities:**

- Review source documents, approvals, immutable ledgers, reports, and audit events.
- Export specifically authorized evidence.

**Restrictions:**

- No create, edit, submit, approve, post, reverse, delete, or configuration actions.
- Sensitive personal and security data must be masked unless explicitly included.
- Access should have an expiry date.

## 6. Data Scope

A role grants actions; a scope limits the records those actions apply to.

Supported scopes should include:

- All company data
- Selected branches
- Selected warehouses and locations
- Selected departments or cost centres
- Selected sales teams or employee groups
- Own records
- Direct reports
- Assigned records
- Date-limited audit scope

The most restrictive applicable scope wins. A user with access to Warehouse A must not receive Warehouse B data through search, reports, exports, notifications, or aggregate totals.

## 7. Sensitive Permissions

The following permissions must be assignable separately:

- View product cost
- View gross margin
- Override sale price or discount
- Sell below cost
- Override credit limit
- Allow negative stock
- Post stock adjustment
- View bank details
- Prepare payment
- Approve payment
- Release payment file
- Post manual journal
- Reopen accounting period
- View employee personal data
- View salary and payroll details
- Run payroll
- Approve payroll
- Export personal or financial data
- Manage API credentials and integrations
- Impersonate or open a support session
- View security logs

## 8. Approval Framework

Approval workflows may depend on:

- Document type
- Amount and currency
- Discount or gross margin
- Supplier, customer, category, or expense type
- Branch, warehouse, department, or cost centre
- Stock variance quantity or value
- Payment method or bank account
- Employee grade or leave type
- Whether the initiator and approver are different users

Each workflow should support:

1. Zero or more sequential approval levels.
2. One or more eligible approvers per level.
3. Any-one or all-required decisions.
4. Delegation with start and end dates.
5. Escalation reminders.
6. Recorded comments and decision timestamps.
7. Rejection to draft or final rejection according to policy.
8. Reapproval when material fields change.

## 9. Segregation of Duties

Recommended controls:

| Process | Duties to separate |
|---|---|
| Purchasing | Request, approve order, receive goods, approve bill, release payment |
| Sales | Create order, approve discount, dispatch goods, receive money, approve refund |
| Inventory | Count stock, approve variance, post adjustment |
| Accounting | Create journal, approve/post journal, reconcile bank, reopen period |
| Expenses | Submit claim, approve claim, pay claim |
| Payroll | Maintain salary, prepare payroll, approve payroll, release bank payment |
| Security | Create admin, assign sensitive role, review access |

Small companies may permit combined duties, but the exception must be explicit, visible in audit reports, and acknowledged by the Company Owner.

## 10. User Lifecycle

### Invitation and Activation

1. An authorized admin enters the email or identity and assigns a role and scope.
2. The system sends a time-limited invitation.
3. The user verifies identity and configures required authentication factors.
4. The system records who invited and activated the user.

### Role or Scope Change

- Changes require an authorized administrator.
- Sensitive grants may require owner approval.
- Existing sessions should be refreshed or revoked when access is reduced.
- The effective change and before/after values are audited.

### Suspension and Offboarding

- Suspension immediately prevents new sessions and posting.
- Active sessions and API tokens are revoked.
- Pending tasks are reassigned.
- Historical records retain the user's identity.
- A user record is deactivated, not removed from business history.

### Emergency Access

- Break-glass access requires a reason and strong authentication.
- Access is time-limited and produces immediate security notifications.
- Every action is audited and reviewed after use.

## 11. Support Access

Support access should follow this sequence:

1. A support case exists.
2. The company approves access when required by policy.
3. An authorized support user requests a limited scope and duration.
4. The platform records the reason and displays a support-session banner.
5. Sensitive fields remain masked unless separately authorized.
6. The session expires automatically.
7. The company can review the access record.

## 12. Permission Administration Rules

- Default roles are templates and may be copied into company-specific roles.
- Protected roles such as Company Owner cannot be deleted.
- No administrator may grant permissions they do not have authority to delegate.
- Role changes must not rewrite historical approval records.
- Permission checks must apply to individual records and bulk actions.
- Exports, dashboards, search indexes, caches, and background jobs use the same scope rules as screens and APIs.
- Denied authorization attempts should be logged when security-relevant.

## Open Business Decisions

1. Can companies create fully custom roles, or only adjust predefined roles?
2. Must every company have exactly one owner, or can ownership be shared?
3. Which actions always require owner approval?
4. Is maker-checker separation mandatory or configurable for small companies?
5. Which approval limits use document currency versus company base currency?
6. Can temporary delegates approve payroll, payments, or security changes?
7. Which employee managers receive team-level attendance and leave permissions?
8. Should auditors be allowed to export, and which fields must be masked?
9. Does support access require company consent for every session?
10. Can Super Admin suspend a company automatically for billing or security reasons?
11. Which user identity providers and multi-factor methods are required?
12. How long should inactive users, sessions, invitations, and access records be retained?
